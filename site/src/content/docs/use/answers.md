---
title: "Understanding Archie's answers"
---

Archie structures its responses consistently so you always know what you're looking at. This page explains each element you'll see and what it means for your decision-making.

## Response structure

Most Archie responses follow a six-part structure. Not every response includes all six parts. A simple policy lookup might only need the first three, while a complex planning request will use all six.

**Summary.** The first thing you'll see is a 2-4 sentence plain-language answer with the key conclusion up front. You should be able to read just this section and understand the answer.

**Confidence signal.** Immediately after the summary, Archie tells you how much to trust this answer. There are three levels, explained below.

**Action steps.** When the answer involves doing something, Archie provides a numbered checklist with what to do, who should do it (by role), and when.

**Templates and artifacts.** When it would save you time, Archie provides ready-to-use documents such as draft letters, meeting agendas, or checklists.

**References.** Every policy claim includes a citation such as *(RI Bylaws §9.030)* or *(Manual of Procedure, p. 42)*.

**Risks and mitigations.** When relevant, Archie flags compliance gaps, common mistakes, or timeline risks with suggested mitigations.

## Confidence signals explained

This is the single most important thing to pay attention to in any Archie response.

> ✅ **Directly supported** means a specific provision in the governing documents answers this question, and Archie can cite the exact section. You can verify the citation yourself, but the answer is grounded in an authoritative source. This is the highest level of confidence.

> ⚠️ **Inferred** means the documents address related topics but don't answer this exact question. Archie's response involves interpretation: combining multiple provisions, applying a general rule to a specific situation, or reasoning by analogy. The answer is reasonable but not guaranteed. Archie will recommend who to verify with and why. Treat inferred answers as a strong starting point, not a final determination.

> ❌ **Not found** means the documents do not address this topic. When you see this, Archie has not attempted an answer. Instead, it tells you what it searched, confirms the answer isn't in the available documents, and directs you to the appropriate human contact. This is not a failure. It is Archie working correctly by refusing to guess.

## Archie's seven modes

Archie automatically selects the right operating mode based on your question. You don't need to specify which mode to use; Archie figures it out from context. Sometimes multiple modes combine for a single request.

| Mode | Activates when you... | Example prompt |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Advisor** | Ask about policy, rules, or compliance | *"Can a club use the Rule of 85 for attendance?"* |
| **Planner** | Need a plan, calendar, or goal framework | *"Help me build a RACI chart for our district goals."* |
| **Coach** | Navigate a difficult conversation or stakeholder situation | *"How should I handle a club president who won't submit reports?"* |
| **Editor** | Need to write, revise, or polish a document | *"Draft a letter to clubs about our new membership initiative."* |
| **Analyst** | Need to compare, summarize, or analyze documents | *"Compare what the RI Bylaws and the Rotary Code of Policies say about attendance."* |
| **Meeting Genie** | Prepare for a meeting, assembly, or event | *"Create an agenda for my next club visit."* |
| **Crisis Helper** | Face an urgent or high-risk situation | *"A reporter is calling about allegations at one of our clubs."* |

## Escalation notices

For certain categories of questions, Archie will not provide substantive guidance. Instead, you'll see an escalation notice identifying the topic, the category (legal, financial, or youth safety), the recommended contacts, and what Archie *can* still help with.

The escalation categories are absolute. Archie will never attempt to answer legal, tax, insurance, youth safety, or financial irregularity questions, even if your question seems straightforward. This boundary exists because AI-generated guidance on these topics, even well-intentioned guidance, creates real liability for your district and real risk for the people involved.

What Archie *can* do during an escalation is help with the surrounding tasks: drafting a message to your district's legal counsel, pulling relevant policy sections for the professional to review, or creating a documentation checklist.

## Verification nudges

For high-stakes answers that aren't in the escalation categories (compliance decisions, governance actions, grant applications), Archie will close its response with a specific verification recommendation. This is not Archie saying it's wrong. It's Archie saying the stakes are high enough that a second opinion from a human expert is worthwhile.

## Asking Archie why it answered that way

If you want to understand Archie's reasoning process, ask **"Why did you answer that way?"** or any similar question. Archie will provide a diagnostic summary showing what it understood your question to be, which policy areas it searched, which documents it drew from, how it resolved any conflicts between sources, and which mode(s) it operated in. This is useful when you want to double-check that Archie interpreted your question correctly.
