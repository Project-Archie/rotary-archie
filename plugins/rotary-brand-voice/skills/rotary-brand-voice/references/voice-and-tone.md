# Rotary voice and tone

Rotary's voice is consistent across print, digital, social, signage, and merchandise. Any single piece should sound unmistakably like Rotary regardless of which club, district, or zone produced it.

Everything not inside a block headed "From the 2014 guidelines (not on the current Brand Center)" comes from the Rotary Brand Center, or is labeled as this skill's own guidance.

## The four attributes

As published (Brand Center: our-brand/brand-elements/voice-and-messaging): "Our voice, the unique tone and style we use to communicate, focuses on four attributes that describe Rotary and our members: **persevering, inspiring, compassionate, and smart.** These attributes set the tone for how we talk about Rotary. Think of them as Rotary's personality."

**Rotary's voice is clear, persuasive, and reliable. It is personal and sincere.**

Applied together, never one at a time. The descriptors and tests below are this skill's working application of the four attributes, not additional Brand Center wording.

### Persevering

Rotary finds lasting solutions to systemic problems. Copy is bold, purposeful, courageous.

- Do: speak with clarity and conviction, and acknowledge how much work is left.
- Don't: hedge, over-qualify, or bury the point in throat-clearing.

### Inspiring

Rotary conveys hope, enthusiasm, and passion, and encourages others to act. Copy is upbeat, hopeful, visionary.

- Do: end with a reason and a way to act.
- Don't: mistake exclamation points for enthusiasm. Confidence carries the tone; punctuation does not.

### Compassionate

Hard problems require empathy. Rotary champions real people and stories that are relatable and universal. Copy is thoughtful, sincere, engaging.

- Do: put people in the copy, named or described, doing something.
- Don't: write about "communities" and "impact" in the abstract, or about beneficiaries as passive recipients.

### Smart

Rotary looks at problems from different angles and applies member expertise where others cannot. Copy is knowledgeable, perceptive, confident.

- Do: name the specific problem, the specific approach, the specific result.
- Don't: write interchangeable nonprofit copy that any charity could have sent.

## Style

- Clear, persuasive, reliable.
- Personal and sincere.
- Collective "we" when speaking on behalf of Rotary.
- Active voice.
- A clear call to action where it fits.

## The core message

**Together, we take action to create lasting change in our communities and around the world.**

That line is Rotary's answer to "What is Rotary?" It creates a personal connection and emphasizes not only the difference Rotary makes but how it happens. Rotary members are effective problem-solvers who create solutions that last because they invest in relationships, mobilize their networks, and keep learning from experience.

**Vision:** together, we see a world where people unite and take action to create lasting change, across the globe, in our communities, and in ourselves.

**Essence:** Rotary joins leaders from all continents, cultures, and occupations to exchange ideas and take action for communities around the world.

**Values** (Brand Center: voice-and-messaging/vision-and-essence): Fellowship, we build lifelong relationships. Integrity, we honor our commitments. Diversity, we connect diverse perspectives. Service and leadership, we use our leadership skills and professional expertise to solve social issues in our communities.

**Action Plan priorities:** increase our impact, expand our reach, enhance participant engagement, increase our ability to adapt.

A piece should clearly express at least one value while applying all four voice attributes. Rotary's Four-Way Test sits alongside the values as the ethical frame; it is not part of the Brand Center voice pages, so cite it as Rotary's guiding principle rather than as brand guidance.

## Proof: six ways to give the audience a reason to believe

Straight from the Brand Center's "How to use our voice." Inspire the audience and give reasons to believe by including:

1. Stories that show club members and participants as people of action making a difference in the community.
2. Clear, compelling facts and data that demonstrate how Rotary contributes to solving problems.
3. Stories about moments when the writer or fellow members recognized the power of Rotary.
4. Descriptions of how members benefited or grew personally or professionally from their Rotary experience.
5. Testimonials from community members or partners that articulate the benefits the club brought to those it served or reached.
6. Notable programs, projects, press coverage, recognition, or partnerships that reinforce Rotary's credibility.

## People of Action

People of Action is not an advertising campaign. It is how Rotary talks about and presents itself, and it is the default frame for a piece about work a club or district actually did. Anchor it in local impact: this club, these members, this project, this result.

**Copy structure for an ad or social post** (Brand Center: our-brand/people-of-action). Keep it short but informative and inspiring, in as few words as possible:

1. **Say what you did.** What action did you take?
2. **Offer statistics or other proof of the difference you made.** What did you accomplish? How did the club's action change lives?
3. **Issue a call to action.** Donate, participate, or learn more. Make it clear what you want people to do.

## Public-facing copy: no Rotary jargon

**Avoid Rotary abbreviations and terminology** for events where nonmembers are likely to be present, and in public-facing messages such as social posts (Brand Center: promote-rotary/events/promoting-an-event). The public-relations page repeats it for letters and opinion pieces: **do not use Rotary jargon.**

Spell out or replace insider terms: DG, AG, PETS, RYLA, RI, TRF, club runner, "make-up," "classification," "fireside." Say what the thing is. Keep the club's own shorthand for member-only communications.

## Channel rules the Brand Center publishes

- **Social posts:** keep accompanying text short and focused on the club's activity, include a call to action with the link, and use the **#PeopleofAction** and **#Rotary** hashtags to strengthen the brand and its visibility (Brand Center: promote-rotary/advertising).
- **Letters to the editor and opinion pieces:** keep them short and simple, **no more than 500 words**, one main message stated in the first sentence, references by date and headline, facts and data behind the stance, contact information, no jargon (Brand Center: promote-rotary/public-relations).
- **News releases:** **one page, written in the third person.** A creative opening draws interest, but the first sentence must be concise, direct, and clear, and the release must cover who, what, where, when, and why.
- **Event messaging:** focus on what the event offers the audience and why they should attend, use straightforward language, and highlight the club's impact in the community.
- **Websites:** show the work with real images, keep it simple because people scan, include clear calls to action, and use Rotary's official colors.

## The annual presidential message

**The 2026-27 message is "Create Lasting Impact."** It builds on "Unite for Good" (2025-26) and encourages members, participants, and the public to take collective action that leads to a sustainable difference, globally, locally, and personally.

**Clubs and districts should use the official presidential message rather than creating their own themes, so the message stays consistent across Rotary. Any other themes a club or district has developed should be retired and replaced by it** (Brand Center: voice-and-messaging/2026-27-ri-presidential-message).

When a draft carries a club or district theme in the place the presidential message belongs, replace it and say so in one sentence. Visual lockup formats for the message are in `logos-and-lockups.md`. The message changes each 1 July, so confirm the current one on the Brand Center before using it on material dated on or after 1 July 2027.

## How to apply: evaluation questions

Run every draft against these four.

1. Does the piece show clarity and conviction, not hedging? (Persevering)
2. Does it convey hope and a call to action, not just facts? (Inspiring)
3. Does it feature real people and warmth, not abstractions? (Compassionate)
4. Does it show Rotary as knowledgeable and confident, not generic? (Smart)

## Recurring failures

These show up over and over in club and district drafts.

| Failure | Fix |
|---|---|
| Exclamation stacking | Cut them. Confidence, not volume. |
| "Rotary family" | Name the people: members, clubs, partners, guests, the community. |
| "Yours in service" style sign-offs | Close in the writer's own plain words. |
| "Service Above Self" as a hype line | Reserve the motto for pieces genuinely about the principle. |
| Rotary jargon in public-facing copy | Spell it out or replace it. |
| A club or district theme where the presidential message belongs | Use "Create Lasting Impact" (2026-27). |
| No call to action, or a vague one ("watch your inbox") | Say what to do, when, and where. |
| Facts with no reason to care | Add the result, the person, or the next step. |
| Em dashes | Banned. Use commas, periods, or colons, and "to" for ranges. |

## From the 2014 guidelines (not on the current Brand Center)

Two before-and-after examples from the 2014 brand sources, lightly rewritten. Useful as craft models; not a Brand Center standard.

### Membership invitation

Generic: "Rotary is ordinary people around the world working together to clean the environment, end polio, improve our communities, and accomplish other extraordinary things. Learn more at rotary.org."

In voice: "It's amazing what we can accomplish when hearts and minds work together. See how leaders from countries, cultures, and occupations around the world are taking action to enhance health, empower youth, promote peace, and improve their community. Join leaders at a Rotary club near you."

Why it works: active, inspiring, and inviting; balances compassion (hearts) with intelligence (minds); defines leadership by mindset and action rather than title; makes the impact concrete; ends with a clear call to action.

### Anniversary copy

Generic: "Rotary's 108th anniversary marks a year of both achievements in the fight to eradicate polio and a stepped-up commitment to finish the job. You can help by learning how to use advocacy to encourage governments to fund the vital work of polio eradication."

In voice: "Another year, another chance to make history. We're closer than ever to eradicating polio. But even as we celebrate our 108th year of community impact, we're reminded that there is much to do. Become an effective advocate for government funding so together we can finish the job."

Why it works: the opening inspires rather than describes; the persevering attribute surfaces in "much to do"; it uses the collective "we"; it ends with a clear call to action.

The 2014 edition also framed three core ideas behind the messaging: Rotary joins leaders, we exchange ideas, we take action. The Brand Center now carries that content as the vision and essence statements above.
