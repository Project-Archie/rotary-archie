# Rotary visual identity

Colors, typography, imagery, and the areas of focus, as published on the Rotary Brand Center (brandcenter.rotary.org). Logos, lockups, and the presidential message live in `logos-and-lockups.md`.

The Brand Center governs. Where a rule below is not on the Brand Center, it is labeled: either as this skill's own guidance or inside a block headed "From the 2014 guidelines (not on the current Brand Center)." Legacy blocks are optional house style, never something Rotary requires. Where the Brand Center is silent (minimum sizes, font weights, lockup spacing), say it is silent and point to the Brand Center template or graphics@rotary.org.

## Colors

Rotary's brand colors create a consistent look across all Rotary communications. The colors are chosen to complement one another and **are used in their pure forms, never altered nor screened** (Brand Center: our-brand/brand-elements/colors).

Each color carries four formulas: Pantone for print, CMYK for 4-color process print, hexadecimal for web, RGB for digital.

Two rules narrow where the palette applies:

- The wider palette is for the elements of your promotional materials, never for the logo. Logo colors come from the template (Brand Center: logos-and-graphics/dos-and-donts, item 5).
- Logo colors cannot be changed. Use only the colors in which the logos are offered (Brand Center: help-and-faq/faq/logos-and-lockups).

### From the 2014 guidelines (not on the current Brand Center)

Optional house guidance. None of this is a Brand Center rule, so offer it only as a suggestion and say where it comes from.

- The 2014 guidelines grouped the palette into leadership colors (Royal Blue, Gold, Azure, Sky Blue), secondary colors (Cranberry, Cardinal, Turquoise, Violet, Orange, Grass), pastels (Slate, Stone, Lavender, Moss, Powder Blue, Taupe), and neutrals (the grays plus black and white).
- Usage advice from that edition: use Azure most often; reserve Royal Blue and Sky Blue to complement and highlight; use Gold as an accent rather than a dominant block; use secondary colors sparingly; use neutrals and white space for balance.
- It also listed a Mist tint and allowed comparable metallic versions on signage, pins, foils, awards, and certificates.

## Typography

The Brand Center publishes five fonts in two roles (Brand Center: our-brand/brand-elements/typography). Use the free options when the licensed options are unavailable or cost-prohibitive.

**Primary fonts, for headlines and navigation labels:**

- Frutiger (licensed)
- Open Sans (free)
- Arial (free)

**Secondary fonts, for body text, secondary headlines, and captions:**

- Sentinel (licensed)
- Georgia (free)

That is the whole published rule. The Brand Center publishes no weights, no condensed requirement, no all-caps requirement, and no split between print and digital, and it never names Open Sans Condensed or Arial Narrow. **Regular Open Sans or regular Arial for headlines and navigation labels is on brand.** Do not tell anyone otherwise, and do not require a condensed or capitalized treatment.

**This skill's own guardrail (not a Rotary rule):** there is no "Rotary Sans" or "Rotary Serif." Do not invent a Rotary typeface, and do not describe a font as Rotary's proprietary face. Use the five published names.

Licensing questions go to graphics@rotary.org.

### From the 2014 guidelines (not on the current Brand Center)

Optional house style for a club or district that wants a tighter system than the Brand Center specifies. Offer it as a choice, never as a requirement.

- Frutiger LT STD weights: condensed (47 Light Condensed, 57 Condensed, 67 Bold Condensed, 77 Black Condensed) and regular (45 Light, 46 Light Italic, 55 Roman, 56 Italic, 65 Bold, 66 Bold Italic, 75 Black, 76 Black Italic, 95 Ultra Black).
- Sentinel weights: Light, Book, Medium, Semibold, Bold, Black, each with an italic.
- The condensed, all-caps headline treatment, with regular weights for subheads, infographics, and dense body copy.
- Open Sans Condensed as the digital stand-in for Frutiger condensed, and Arial Narrow as the Microsoft Office stand-in.
- The rule against mixing licensed and free fonts within a single piece.
- Type craft that still travels well: keep leading and kerning consistent, use lighter weights for body copy, reserve bold and italic for emphasis, and give colored type sufficient contrast.

## Imagery

The Brand Center's photography statement (Brand Center: our-brand/brand-elements/images-and-videos): Rotary photography focuses on relationships, community impact, and action and engagement. Photos should always tell a genuine story. They should be powerful and evocative images that inspire and motivate the public and show that Rotary is made up of people of action. A documentary style, meaning the subjects are not posed, usually accomplishes this best.

**People of Action selection criteria** (Brand Center: our-brand/people-of-action). Choose images that show Rotary participants taking action by:

- Featuring real-life, everyday moments
- Displaying a sense of motion
- Featuring just a few people that viewers can see well
- Using warm lighting and natural colors
- Representing the diversity of your club and community

**Websites** (Brand Center: promote-rotary/websites): photos should focus on relationships and community impact and always tell a genuine story. Whenever possible, use images that show multiple Rotary members and reflect Rotary's diversity. Always get signed written permission from anyone pictured in photos or videos used for Rotary purposes.

Video guidance on the same page: be upbeat and show members as people of action, identify local needs and show the club working with the community, and show rather than tell.

**Permissions are a hard gate.** Signed written releases, minors, and copyright ownership are covered in `ai-use-rules.md` under "Photo permissions," alongside the Rotary International AI Guidelines material on likenesses. Read it before advising on any photo or video.

### From the 2014 guidelines (not on the current Brand Center)

Optional art direction. Useful when briefing a photographer, but not a Rotary requirement.

- Subject categories: Rotarians uniting and exchanging ideas; Rotarians taking action for the community, with or without beneficiaries present; metaphorical or conceptual images such as hands, motion, and symbolic objects.
- Style notes: editorial or reportage, fly on the wall, candid poses, real expressions, camaraderie and momentum, short depth of field with selective focus, wide angles, natural backgrounds rather than studio backdrops.
- Indoors, keep backgrounds blurry or nondescript and play down drop ceilings, dated fixtures, and exit signs.
- Beneficiaries should be actively engaged or visible as participants, never incidental observers in the background.

## Areas of focus

Rotary has seven areas of focus. Each one has a specific icon, a title (the label), and an **official color** (Brand Center: logos-and-graphics/areas-of-focus-icons).

| # | Area of focus | Color | Hex |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Peacebuilding and conflict prevention | Azure | `#0067c8` |
| 2 | Disease prevention and treatment | Cardinal | `#e02927` |
| 3 | Water, sanitation, and hygiene | Sky Blue | `#00a2e0` |
| 4 | Maternal and child health | Violet | `#901f93` |
| 5 | Basic education and literacy | Orange | `#ff7600` |
| 6 | Community economic development | Turquoise | `#00adbb` |
| 7 | Environment | Grass | `#009739` |

Rules as published:

- **Grouped icons always appear in the order above.** They can be used with or without their labels.
- Grouped icons are available arranged **horizontally, vertically, and in a circle**. Choose the arrangement that works best for the design.
- **Black or white versions** are available when the color icons are not suitable for the materials. Choose the one that gives the highest contrast so the icons stay easy to identify.
- For an **individual** icon, the label is optional. When used, it appears either under the icon or to the right of it.
- **The club, district, or zone logo must appear in the design alongside the icons.**
- **The icons cannot be altered or manipulated in any way.**
- Icon files come from the Brand Center asset library.

**A superseded rule to watch for.** Older material, including the 2014 guidelines, says to show the seven icons in one neutral color and never to color-code them individually. That is superseded. The Brand Center assigns each area an official color and a fixed order. If a request repeats the neutral-only rule, correct it. If a user asks for the seven icons in their own colors, that request is correct as written.

### From the 2014 guidelines (not on the current Brand Center)

- The neutral-color rule described above: all seven in one Rotary neutral when shown together, one highlighted in a leadership or secondary color when a single area is the subject. Superseded, do not apply it.
- Minimum sizes from that edition: print 13mm or 0.5 inches, digital 60 pixels on desktop and 80 pixels on mobile, with 3mm or 0.125 inches between icons. The Brand Center publishes no minimum size for the icons. If a size floor is needed, say the Brand Center is silent and offer these as legacy figures, or point to the Brand Center asset files and graphics@rotary.org.
