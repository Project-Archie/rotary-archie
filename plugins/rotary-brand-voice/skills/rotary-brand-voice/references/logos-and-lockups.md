# Logos and lockups

Every logo rule below comes from the Rotary Brand Center (brandcenter.rotary.org) unless it sits inside a block headed "From the 2014 guidelines (not on the current Brand Center)." Those blocks are optional house guidance, never a Rotary requirement.

This skill bundles no logo files. They are trademarked, licensed assets. Files and templates come from the Brand Center, or from graphics@rotary.org. Never generate, draw, trace, or reconstruct a Rotary mark: see `ai-use-rules.md`.

## Masterbrand Signature

The Rotary, Rotaract, and Interact Masterbrand Signatures are the official logos of Rotary International. Each combines the wheel with the word "Rotary," "Rotaract," or "Interact" **to the left** of the wheel, for visibility and recognition (Brand Center: logos-and-graphics/rotary-logos).

- **The Masterbrand Signature cannot be altered in any way.**
- **Use the club, district, or zone logo rather than the Masterbrand Signature alone** when promoting a club or another Rotary entity or its activities. A bare signature can wrongly imply that Rotary International organizes or manages the project or event.
- A club should not use the Masterbrand Signature or its club logo for any project or event controlled by another organization.
- The same rules apply to the Rotaract and Interact logos.
- **Regular or Simplified:** either version may be used on all print and digital applications. The Brand Center **recommends the Simplified version if the logo will be smaller than 1.25 cm (0.5 inches) or will be embroidered** (also stated in help-and-faq/faq/logos-and-lockups).

## Mark of Excellence

The Rotary wheel on its own is the Mark of Excellence: a **secondary** logo (Brand Center: logos-and-graphics/rotary-logos).

- Mainly used on round items such as road signs and merchandise.
- May be the round profile image on social media pages for a Rotary club, district, zone, Rotary Fellowship, or Rotary Action Group, when the page is identified as that entity (Brand Center: help-and-faq/faq/logos-and-lockups).
- On items such as flyers or banners, it must sit **near the club or district logo**.
- **Never alone, and never a club's official logo.**
- **There is no simplified version.** The words "Rotary International" always appear in the wheel.
- Use only the Mark of Excellence available from the Brand Center. Do not alter it to represent another object.

## Club, district, and zone logos

A club, district, or zone adds its name to the Rotary Masterbrand Signature (Brand Center: logos-and-graphics/logos-for-clubs-districts-and-zones).

- **Use the Brand Center template, or follow its format exactly.** All older club logos must be retired.
- District and zone logos carry only the word "District" or "Zone" and the corresponding number.
- Rotaract and Interact club logos follow the same structure.
- Use the club name on the charter, or the name the club is known by in the community. **"Club" is optional:** "Rotary Springfield" works as well as "Rotary Club of Springfield." Avoid a name that could mislead people about what the club is.
- Because "Rotary," "Rotaract," or "Interact" is already in the signature, do not repeat it. The Rotary Club of Evanston adds only "Club of Evanston."
- The club name **always appears on the same side as the wordmark**, and **aligns on the right with the "y" in "Rotary" or the final "t" in "Rotaract" or "Interact."** Wording may continue onto a second line for visual balance.
- **The font, color, position, and the size relationship between the name and the wheel cannot be altered.**
- The official club logo is the Masterbrand Signature plus the club name and **nothing else**. Do not add other text or graphics, and do not make the logo a subordinate part of a larger design.
- Either the Regular or the Simplified signature may be used to build the club logo, with the same Simplified recommendation under 1.25 cm (0.5 inches) or for embroidery.
- **The club name never sits to the right of the wheel.** That space is reserved for a partner, sponsor, program, or event (Brand Center: help-and-faq/faq/logos-and-lockups).
- **A historic club logo** may survive only as a **secondary** mark, kept apart from the official club logo, and only if it contains no Rotary logos.
- **Rotaract multidistrict information organizations:** an MDIO logo follows the club and district structure but must include the organization name or the abbreviation "MDIO" plus the location or region.
- Club names may be set **in the club's own language**; the template accommodates it. **Never translate the word "Rotary"** (Brand Center: logos-and-graphics/dos-and-donts).

### Geographic identifiers

When clubs, districts, or zones work together on an activity or project, a geographic identifier may replace the club, district, or zone name, with permissions and conditions.

Conditions:

- The identifier must include the word "clubs" or "districts."
- Decide how long the identifier will be used, document the plan, keep it accessible to members, and provide it to Rotary International or local leaders on request.
- Any activity or project using the identifier must be under the full control of a Rotary entity, administered by that entity and not delegated (Rotary Code of Policies 34.040.7).
- Do not use the identifier in place of the formal name or logo for official business, including charitable registrations and contracts.

Permissions ladder, by area:

| Area | Permission from |
|---|---|
| An area within a district, such as a city or region | All club presidents |
| A country within a multi-country district | All club presidents |
| A whole country, all districts within it | All district governors |
| Several districts, such as a county or region | All district governors |
| A zone or Intercountry Committee | The RI director or directors |

If a new person in a role objects, the identifier can no longer be used.

Limits: no incorporation or trademark or business-name registration using the identifier unless Rotary policy permits; never a substitute for the official logo; Rotary International can block use if the guidelines are not followed fully or the identifier was chosen in bad faith; using one creates no new Rotary entity, confers no additional rights to Rotary marks, and grants no right to transfer them.

## Clear space and size

- **Clear space:** show the logo in its entirety with space around it equal to the height of the capital "R" in the Rotary or Rotaract wordmark, or the capital "I" in the Interact wordmark (Brand Center: logos-and-graphics/dos-and-donts, item 4). This is the only clear-space measure the Brand Center publishes.
- **Minimum size:** the Brand Center publishes none. If a floor is needed, say the Brand Center is silent, use the Simplified recommendation threshold (1.25 cm or 0.5 inches) as the practical guide for choosing Regular versus Simplified, and point to the Brand Center template or graphics@rotary.org.

## The lockup system

A lockup is a combination of graphic elements that shows an affiliation between a club, district, or zone and **one** other entity: a Rotary program or activity, a partner or sponsor, or an event (Brand Center: logos-and-graphics/logos-for-rotary-programs-and-activities, logos-for-partners, logos-for-events-and-projects).

The shape is always the same: **club, district, or zone logo on the left, a vertical line, one entity on the right.** Build it from the Brand Center lockup template.

Rules that hold across every lockup type:

- Use the club, district, or zone logo, **not** the Masterbrand Signature, so it is clear that the club and not Rotary International is the partner or participant.
- **One entity only.** One partner, sponsor, program, or event per lockup. Do not add vertical lines to fit more.
- A graphic representing the other entity **must not include or resemble the Masterbrand Signature or the Mark of Excellence.**
- **Rotary logos are never placed inside, nested in, or made a subordinate part of another logo.**
- Do not use the lockup format for club or district committee names, for regional nicknames, for original graphics, or in any way not specified in the Brand Center.
- Do not add descriptive text or unrelated logos to a lockup. Put that information elsewhere in the design, away from the logo.

### Programs and activities

There is a graphic-versus-text asymmetry across program types:

| Program | Right side of the line |
|---|---|
| Rotary Youth Exchange, Rotary Youth Leadership Awards (RYLA) | Text **or** a graphic |
| Rotary Friendship Exchange, Rotary Community Corps, intercountry committees, project fairs | **Text only** |
| Alumni associations and Rotex | Text **or** a graphic, locked up with a **district, multidistrict, or zone** logo |

RYE and RYLA lockups use club, district, or multidistrict logos on the left. The Rotary Friendship Exchange, Rotary Community Corps, and intercountry committee program logos were retired in 2013; promote those programs with a lockup instead (Brand Center: help-and-faq/faq/logos-and-lockups).

Rotary Action Groups and Rotary Fellowships have their own detailed visual identity guidance in My Rotary, and build their official logo from the same lockup template.

### Partners and sponsors

- A lockup may promote an event or activity run with a local partner or sponsor: club, district, or multidistrict logo, vertical line, partner or sponsor name or graphic.
- **Get the partner's permission to use its logo before creating the lockup.**
- **Only one partner or sponsor logo can be used in a lockup.** Other partner logos go elsewhere in the materials, away from the lockup.
- **If all partners are equal, do not use a lockup at all.** List their logos separately from the official logo.
- Another organization's trademarked logo may appear in a lockup with the club logo. It may not contain or resemble the wheel or signature, and Rotary marks are never nested inside it.
- A club, district, or zone may also lock up with Rotary initiatives such as End Polio Now and People of Action.

### Events and projects

Use the lockup template to create a custom logo for an event or project: club, district, or zone logo, vertical line, the event or project name or graphic on the right. A custom graphic must not include or resemble the Masterbrand Signature or the Mark of Excellence. There is no such thing as a freestanding Rotary event badge that carries the wheel.

More than one club, district, or zone name may appear in a logo to represent a collaboration; a geographic identifier covers the case where all clubs or districts in an area are involved.

### End Polio Now

Two logos (Brand Center: logos-and-graphics/end-polio-now-logo):

- **Primary logo.** Includes the full wheel, which cannot be replaced with the simplified version because it must show the words "Rotary International." The red box is part of the logo; printed on a red background, the "o" will look truncated. **Usable only when the materials also include the club, district, or zone logo or name.**
- **Secondary logo.** Square, no wheel. Use it **in lockups** and wherever the primary logo will not work. Same condition: the club, district, or zone logo or name must also be present.
- **Lockup:** the secondary End Polio Now logo with the club, district, or zone logo, to show affiliation with the polio eradication effort at an event, a fundraiser, or in communications.
- **For any polio-related event or fundraiser, offer this lockup first.** It is the Brand Center's own mechanism for the affiliation. A custom event lockup (club logo, line, event name or graphic) is the alternative, and a piece may carry both, kept apart.

### Presidential message

The annual RI presidential message is available as a lockup with the club, district, or zone logo (Brand Center: voice-and-messaging/2026-27-ri-presidential-message).

- **The 2026-27 message is "Create Lasting Impact."** It follows "Unite for Good" (2025-26).
- **Clubs and districts should use the official presidential message rather than creating their own themes.** Any other themes a club or district has developed **should be retired and replaced** by the annual presidential message.
- This is not limited to an official theme slot. A homegrown theme used as a tagline, caption, subhead, hashtag, or jersey line on any club or district material is the same theme, and it is retired the same way. When a request carries one, replace it with the presidential message or drop the line, and say so in one sentence. "This is just a caption" is not an exception the Brand Center makes, and neither is "we will use it on our other materials instead." Retired means retired across the club's or district's communications, not moved to a different piece. Do not offer another home for the theme.
- Two lockup formats: a **graphic format** (club, district, or zone logo plus the message graphic, selected from the drop-down in the lockup template) and a **text format** (the message typed into the template's text box).
- Typical placements: club and district newsletters and presentations, Rotary email signatures, district conference materials such as badges and invitations, online meeting backgrounds, and social or blog posts.
- Ready-made presidential-message graphics for websites and social media are in the Brand Center asset library.
- Merchandise carrying the presidential message comes from Rotary's approved licensed vendors. The presidential message and the Rotary logo are protected trademarks of Rotary International.

**Rollover note.** The presidential message changes each 1 July. "Create Lasting Impact" is the 2026-27 message. Before applying it to material dated on or after 1 July 2027, confirm the current message on the Brand Center rather than assuming this one still stands.

## The 13 do's and don'ts

Near-verbatim from Brand Center: logos-and-graphics/dos-and-donts.

| # | Correct | Incorrect |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Use your club, district, or zone logo as your official logo | Do not use the Rotary, Rotaract, or Interact Masterbrand Signature without your club, district, or zone name |
| 2 | Use the template to create club and district logos so everything is in the proper place | Do not move around or manipulate the components of the Rotary, Rotaract, or Interact logo |
| 3 | Use the official club logo, club name plus Masterbrand Signature, as the main identifier in all promotional materials | Do not alter the Mark of Excellence to represent another object, change the color or font of the club name, or add other images to the club logo |
| 4 | Show the logo in its entirety with enough space around it: the height of the capital "R" in the Rotary or Rotaract wordmark, or the capital "I" in the Interact wordmark | Do not use only part of the logo, obscure any part of it, or use elements of it in another logo |
| 5 | Use only the brand colors specified for the Mark of Excellence, the Masterbrand Signature, and the club name; the templates give the right colors automatically | Do not use colors in the logo that are not part of the template. The other brand colors in the palette are for different elements of promotional materials |
| 6 | Use the Mark of Excellence near the club or district logo for clarity and recognition | Do not use the Mark of Excellence alone or as the club's official logo. Use only the Brand Center version. There is no simplified version |
| 7 | Use the lockup templates to reflect the club or district's relationship with **one** partner, sponsor, event, or program; list several partners or co-sponsors elsewhere in the materials | Do not add more vertical lines to the lockup template to include more partners, programs, events, or sponsors |
| 8 | Add only the name of the club, district, zone, or official Rotary entity to the Masterbrand Signature | Do not add extra descriptive text, club mottos, event themes, or other wording to the logo. Put that information elsewhere in the design |
| 9 | Use the lockup template to add the names or logos of partners, sponsors, events, or programs | Do not add descriptive text or other logos unrelated to that partner, sponsor, event, or program |
| 10 | Use Rotary's current branding and logos | Do not use previous versions of the Rotary logo. The only exception is a historic photo illustrating a story about events before July 2013 |
| 11 | Use more than one name in a logo to represent a collaboration of many clubs, districts, or zones for an event or project, or a geographic identifier when all clubs or districts in an area are involved | Do not use the Masterbrand Signature on its own, which implies Rotary International organizes or leads the project |
| 12 | Add the club name in the club's own language; the template accommodates it | Do not change the word "Rotary" into another language |
| 13 | When dedicating a place or object in the community, include the club, district, or zone logo on a nearby sign or material, and apply for licensing approval first | Do not use the retired Rotary logo (the wheel alone) or current brand elements without identifying the club, district, or zone |

## Templates, approval, and licensing

- **Templates.** Club, district, and zone logos, Interact and Rotaract logos, and logo lockups are all built from customizable Brand Center templates (Brand Center: templates). Event flyer and People of Action templates live there too.
- **There is no RI logo approval process.** Rotary International does not approve club, district, or zone logos. The club or district public image chair can help, and the Learning Center course "Our Logo: Representing Rotary" covers the rules.
- **Updating a club's materials:** start with what the public sees, the club website, social media channels, and promotional materials.
- **Licensing.** Only Rotary International licensees may produce and sell merchandise bearing the Rotary Marks (Brand Center: promote-rotary/merchandise). A club, district, Rotary Fellowship, or Rotary Action Group that wants to sell merchandise with its logo, or to use an unlicensed vendor for an unavailable item, contacts RI Licensing Services (rilicensingservices@rotary.org) for a license, an exception, or one-time design approval. Dedications of a place or object need licensing approval before you begin.
- **Trading banners.** Position the club, district, or zone logo in the **top right corner**, with custom illustrations and graphics below it. Have an RI-licensed vendor produce the banners.
- **Websites.** Put the club, district, or zone logo in the site header and footer. Either the Regular or the Simplified version may be used (Brand Center: promote-rotary/websites).
- **Social media.** Use the official club, district, or zone logo on the account or page. The Mark of Excellence may be the round profile image for an identified Rotary entity.

## From the 2014 guidelines (not on the current Brand Center)

Optional house guidance only, and superseded wherever it conflicts with the Brand Center rules above. Never present any of it as a Rotary requirement.

- **Minimum sizes:** signature print minimum 13mm or 0.5 inches wide; digital minimum 60 pixels on desktop and 80 pixels on mobile or tablet; the simplified signature capped at 60 pixels desktop and 80 pixels mobile with floors of 30 and 40 pixels. The Brand Center publishes no minimum size.
- **Digital clear space** equal to the height of the lowercase "o" in the wordmark. The Brand Center publishes only the capital "R" or capital "I" measure, and applies it generally.
- **Mark of Excellence sizing:** at least four times the height of the wheel in any nearby Masterbrand Signature, with a 52mm (2 inch) print minimum when paired, reserved for covers and single-page pieces, not repeated within a piece. The Brand Center instead frames it as a secondary logo for round items, merchandise, and social profile images, kept near the club logo.
- **Partner lockup mechanics:** partner logo height at least equal to and no more than 1.5 times the Rotary logo height, a 0.5pt divider rule at 50 percent black or white, divider height matching the first partner logo, consistent spacing between multiple partner logos. The Brand Center specifies only a vertical line from the lockup template and one entity.
- **Club name typography:** Frutiger LT STD 45 Light at 12pt in Rotary Royal Blue. The Brand Center says the template's font, color, position, and size relationship cannot be altered, and publishes no point size.
- **File formats by application:** .eps in spot or CMYK for print, embroidery, and silkscreen; .png in RGB for Word, PowerPoint, web, email, tablet, and mobile.
- The Rotary member pin was unaffected by the 2014 brand work. Current pins and those from licensed suppliers remain acceptable.
